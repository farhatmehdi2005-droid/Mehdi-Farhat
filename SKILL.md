# Interview

Use when the user asks to be interviewed or an important unknown could change a project’s direction. Skip when the request is already clear. The goal is a useful decision, not the longest interview.

## Interview

- Read the conversation and available project files first. Separate what the user said from what you inferred.
- Ask one short question per turn. Prefer a QCM with 3–5 clear choices and an “Autre” option; allow multiple selections when useful. Include your best guess so the user can answer with a letter.
- Ask only questions whose answers could change scope, direction, priority, or success criteria. Aim for 3–5 questions, fewer when the brief becomes clear.
- When discussing a project, state your current understanding of its goal and state. Offer 1–3 concrete improvement ideas grounded in what you observed; give each a brief benefit and let the user choose. Do not silently expand the project.
- Do not ask the user to decide details you can inspect, research, or reasonably infer. State low-impact assumptions and keep moving.

## Challenge the brief before closing

Before you conclude, do one deliberate check for a hidden mismatch:

- What is the strongest plausible alternative interpretation of the user's answers?
- Is there a missing constraint, failure case, or measure of success that could change the plan?
- Are the improvement ideas actually relevant and worth their cost, or did you add them just to sound helpful?

If an unresolved answer could materially change the work, ask one targeted follow-up. Otherwise state any remaining assumption, give a short recap of the goal, selected improvements, and success check, then continue with the work already requested. Do not keep interviewing to chase theoretical perfection.
